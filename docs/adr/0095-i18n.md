# ADR 0095: The screens in two languages

## Context

[ADR 0092](0092-one-point-zero-hardening-plan.md) put "user documentation complete in English and a Portuguese translation of the UI" in
Phase 9 and said what to settle here. What the web app is today: **every text is Portuguese and written in place** (about 30,000
lines of components, 46 pages, and the tables of labels in modules such as the navigation, the roles and the creation menu). There
is no translation layer. The server's messages (`{"error": "..."}`) are English already and are shown as they come; the e-mails and the
PDFs the server writes are Portuguese and are not part of the screens.

The people who use the app today read Portuguese, and 1.0 is the first release others will read the code and the docs of. So the source
should be English (what a contributor reads), the product should not change for anyone who uses it, and English must be a thing a
person can choose.

## Options considered

| Question | Options | Chosen, and why |
|----------|---------|-----------------|
| The library | `react-i18next`, `react-intl`/FormatJS, Lingui, a small layer of our own | **Our own.** What the screens need is a lookup by key, `{name}` parts and, in a few sentences, a link or a bold word in the middle. That is 100 lines (`web/src/i18n/index.tsx`); the libraries bring ICU message syntax, plural engines and extraction toolchains this app has no use for, at tens of kilobytes the PWA caches and a build step to maintain. Revisit when a language with real plural rules or a third language arrives |
| The key | An opaque id (`shell.footer.source`), or the **English text itself** | **The English text.** The source reads as the screen reads; a missing translation falls back to a sentence a person understands, not to an id; there is no key to invent. The cost is that changing a sentence changes its key, which the parity test catches |
| The default language | Portuguese, English, or the browser's | **Portuguese (pt-BR).** It is what every present user sees, so no test, habit or screenshot changes; the product serves Brazilian small businesses. The browser's language is not a first guess: an English browser on a Brazilian business tool is the case least worth surprising. English is one choice away |
| Where the choice is remembered | In the account on the server, or on the device | **On the device, per person**: `conta-verde.language.{user id}` in `localStorage`, plus `conta-verde.language` for what was last chosen on the device (it serves the sign-in pages and a person who never chose). It needs no endpoint, no migration and no audit; it does not follow a person to another device, which an account setting would. Blocked storage means the default, and the choice lasts until the page is closed |
| Dates, numbers and money | Follow the language, or stay as they are | **Stay Brazilian** (`dd/mm/aaaa`, `1.234,56`, `R$`): the books are in reais and the formatters are shared by exports and reports. The language switches words, not the format of figures |

## Decision

- `web/src/i18n/index.tsx`: `I18nProvider` (inside the session, because the choice is per person), `useT()` giving `t("English text", {name})`,
  `useI18n()` giving also `rich("… {link} …", {link: <a/>})` for a sentence that holds a node, `msg("English text")` that returns its argument and
  marks a text written where no component can translate it (a table of labels in a module): the place that shows it calls `t` on it.
  Outside a provider (a test of one component) the default language is used.
- **The catalogue** is `web/src/i18n/pt-BR/`: one file per area, each a `Record<English text, Portuguese text>`, merged in `index.ts`, so the
  pull requests that translate the areas do not meet in one file. English needs no catalogue: its text *is* the key.
- **The checks** (`web/src/i18n/catalogue.test.ts`) read every source file and find every `t("…")`, `rich("…")` and `msg("…")` with a literal:
  every one must be in the catalogue, the catalogue must hold nothing the screens do not use, no key may be given by two files, no translation
  may be empty, and a translation must have the same `{names}` as its key. `i18n.test.tsx` renders the pages in both languages, holds the
  switch and the memory per person, and that no English key shows on a Portuguese screen.
- **How a screen is converted** (the way the next pull requests go): write the English in `t("…")` where the Portuguese was, put the Portuguese
  that was there in the area's catalogue file under that key, add `const t = useT()`; a sentence with a link or a bold word is one `rich` key with
  `{name}` where the node goes (never two keys cut in the middle); a constant outside a component uses `msg` and its user calls `t`; a text with a
  number or a name takes a `{part}`, never a template string. Because the default is Portuguese and the Portuguese is moved, **the existing
  tests pass unchanged**: a conversion that makes one fail changed a text.
- The language switch is in the top bar of the signed-in screens (`LanguageSwitch`). `document.documentElement.lang` follows it.

### This pull request and the ones after

This one is the layer and the first screens: the footer, the sign-in and sign-up pages, the company picker, the shell (the top bar, the creation
menu, the navigation, the layout), the roles and the e-mail confirmation banner, with their catalogue files (`shell.ts`, `auth.ts`). **Every
other page is still Portuguese in both languages** until its pull request, one area at a time, in this order: registers (parties, products,
the financial registers), finance, sales and collection, purchases and stock, the accountant and fiscal, integrations and administration,
then the creation sheets and the shared components that still hold text. The roadmap line stays open until the last of them lands and the
checks above hold for the whole app.

## Consequences

- A page not yet converted mixes languages when English is chosen (its own texts stay Portuguese, the shell is English). That is the price of
  converting by area; the roadmap says how far it got.
- Server-written texts (the e-mails, the quote and payment PDFs, the Portuguese reminders) do not follow the language: they are the company's
  words to its customers, and a customer's language is not the user's.
- The parity test depends on literals: a key built with a variable (`t(prefix + "…")`) is invisible to it and is forbidden by the conversion rule.
- A new screen is written in English with `t`, and its Portuguese goes in the catalogue in the same pull request, or the test fails.

## Revisit when

A third language is asked for (plural rules, a library, extraction), a person asks to have the language follow the account across devices, or
the formats of dates and numbers must follow the language.
